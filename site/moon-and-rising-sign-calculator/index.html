WHEN TIME IS MISSING

Do not turn an incomplete record into a confident label.

Moon stays in one sign

This page checks the first and last local minute of the date. If both return the same sign, the Moon result is date-stable for that offset.

Moon changes sign that day

The page lists both signs and keeps the Moon unresolved until a recorded time is available.

Rising sign

The Ascendant remains unresolved without a time, UTC offset, latitude, and longitude. Noon is never substituted.

WHY SERVICES DISAGREE

Check what the result actually required.

Some Big Three tools collect a name, city, and exact time before they reveal anything. Others allow a no-time entry but limit the output. Neither form alone tells you whether a label is established. The useful distinction is whether the calculation gives the same Moon sign across the date and whether the recorded moment is sufficient for the horizon calculation.

This page shows that distinction beside the result, using a tropical zodiac, Swiss Ephemeris planetary positions, and a Placidus Ascendant when the required inputs exist.

DIRECT ANSWERS

Before you interpret the Big Three

Can I find my Moon and Rising signs without a birth time?

You may be able to confirm the Moon if it stays in one sign for your whole local birth date; a Rising sign still needs a recorded time, UTC offset, and coordinates.

Why is a city field not enough for a Rising sign?

A city name is not a calculation setting. The Ascendant needs coordinates and the historical UTC offset, as well as a time.

Does this replace a full birth chart?

No. Use the natal chart calculator for a broader chart view after checking the birth inputs.
