A record-first result

Find your rising sign
without guessing the time.

To find your rising sign, calculate with your recorded birth date, local birth time and birth city; the result is only as certain as that time record. A missing or approximate time can still support a limited comparison, but it does not justify choosing one Ascendant label as confirmed.

Last updated November 5, 2026

Three fields make the result

Local date

Use the date where the birth happened. Do not translate it to today’s time zone before calculating.

Recorded time

Keep the source wording: exact, approximate, conflicting, or unknown. Each leads to a different confidence level.

Birth city

Enter the actual city in the record so the calculator can apply its location and time-zone rules.

This page’s original contribution is the confidence label beside the rising result: record whether the time is exact, approximate, conflicting or unknown at the moment you save the sign. That small label prevents a calculator output from outgrowing the record that produced it.

Choose the result your record supports

Exact time

Calculate once, save the input fields and visible settings, then read the displayed Ascendant as the result of that stated record.

Approximate time

Run two realistic nearby times. Keep the rising sign only if both outputs agree; otherwise preserve both possible results.

Conflicting times

Keep both source times visible. A preferred interpretation is not evidence for selecting one source over the other.

Unknown time

Do not assign a rising sign. Date-based placements can be read separately, but houses and the Ascendant remain unresolved.

One useful follow-up

Write one line under the result: “Calculated from [source type] time, [city], [exact/approximate/unknown].” This makes it possible to revisit the result when a better record appears, instead of treating an interface label as a permanent fact.

Once the calculation is supported, read the interpretation that matches the result, such as Leo Rising, Virgo Rising, Libra Rising, Sagittarius Rising, Gemini Rising, Capricorn Rising, Taurus Rising, or Cancer Rising, as a focused reflection rather than a replacement for the input record.

What a rising sign does not settle

A calculated Ascendant is an astrology placement under selected inputs. It does not establish personality, explain another person’s behavior, or predict an event. Use it as an interpretive reference only after the record is clear.

Find-your-rising-sign questions

Can I find my rising sign without a birth time?

No confirmed rising sign can be calculated without a sufficiently recorded local time. Keep the Ascendant unresolved rather than selecting a likely label.

Why does a calculator ask for my birth city?

The rising point depends on the location and moment used in the calculation, so the city is part of the input record rather than optional decoration.

What if two calculators disagree?

Compare the date, time, city, time-zone treatment and visible settings before interpreting the labels. Preserve the disagreement if the inputs or models are different.

Is my rising sign the same as my Sun sign?

No. A Sun-sign label is based on solar position; the Ascendant is time- and location-sensitive.
