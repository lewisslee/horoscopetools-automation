Stellium Guide · Transparent Rules

Do I Have a Stellium?

You probably have a stellium when three or more planets occupy the same zodiac sign or astrological house. Some astrologers require four planets, or three when the Sun and Mercury are both included. Check both definitions, then inspect the planets' actual degree distances instead of relying on a label alone.

3+ planetsBroad modern rule; useful for finding concentrated chart themes.
4+ planetsStricter rule; reduces clusters created by common Sun–Mercury–Venus proximity.
Sign ≠ houseA sign stellium describes style. A house stellium describes the life area receiving the concentration.

Published September 28, 2026 · Educational astrology guide

The five-step stellium check

  1. Generate a natal chart with an accurate birth date, place, and time. The date is enough to locate most planets by sign. Houses and the Ascendant need a reliable time.
  2. Write down the sign and house of every planet. Count the Sun through Pluto first. Keep the lunar nodes, asteroids, angles, and calculated points in a separate list.
  3. Circle any sign or house containing three or more planets. This is a candidate stellium under the broad rule, not yet a final judgment.
  4. Run the strict check. Does the cluster contain four planets? If it contains three, does it still qualify when you remove one of the Sun, Mercury, or Venus?
  5. Compare their degrees. Planets spread across nearly 30° share a sign but may not operate like one tight conjunction. Record the widest gap and the closest pairs.

Why calculators can disagree

There is no single governing body that fixes one universal stellium definition. A result should therefore disclose its rule. A tool that prints “stellium” without showing the counted planets, their locations, and its threshold hides the most useful part of the calculation.

RuleCounts as a stelliumBest use
BroadThree or more planets in one sign or houseFinding areas of obvious concentration
StrictFour or more planets in one sign or houseAvoiding an overcount from frequently close planets
Conjunction basedThree or more planets connected by sufficiently close angular distancesMeasuring how tightly the planets act as a unit

Worked example: one chart, two answers

Imagine a chart with the Sun at 4° Libra, Mercury at 10° Libra, Venus at 26° Libra, and every other planet in a different sign.

CheckResultReason
Broad sign ruleYesThree planets are in Libra.
Strict four-planet ruleNoOnly three planets occupy Libra.
Remove Sun or MercuryNoOnly two planets remain, showing why some readers use a stricter threshold.
Tightness checkMixedSun and Mercury are 6° apart; Venus sits much farther away.

The transparent conclusion is not “the calculator is wrong.” The chart meets the broad rule but not the strict rule, and its closest pair is more tightly connected than the full group. That statement preserves the actual data.

Sign stellium or house stellium?

A sign stellium concentrates several planetary functions through one sign's style. A house stellium concentrates them in one life area. The same planets may form both, only one, or neither. Near a house cusp, planets in the same sign can fall into adjacent houses. With some house systems, a single house can also contain planets from two signs.

Read the sign as how the concentration tends to express itself and the house as where it becomes most visible. Do not replace the individual planets with a generic sign description: Mars, Saturn, and Neptune grouped together present different tensions from the Sun, Mercury, and Venus.

What if I do not know my birth time?

You can usually check a sign stellium without a birth time, although the Moon may change signs on some dates. You cannot reliably claim a house stellium without a dependable time because the house cusps move through the day. Record the result as “sign only” until a time source is available.

  • If the Moon changes signs that day, test the earliest and latest plausible times.
  • Do not use a default noon chart to make a confident house claim.
  • Label an estimated time as estimated. Precision in the display does not make the input accurate.

How to interpret the result without exaggerating it

A stellium shows concentration, not automatic talent, disaster, or destiny. Start with the planets: what functions are gathered? Then add the sign, house, and close aspects. Finally, look for balancing factors elsewhere in the chart.

Use three practical questions: Which needs compete inside the cluster? Where does repeated attention produce skill? Which neglected area would add balance? These questions produce a more specific reading than treating every person with the same sign stellium as identical.

Quick answers

Do the Sun and Moon count?

They usually count as planets in astrological shorthand. Because the Sun, Mercury, and Venus often travel near one another, some astrologers ask for a fourth body before calling that trio a stellium.

Do Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto count?

They can count, but people born across a long span may share their sign placement. A cluster containing personal planets is more individually distinctive than an outer-planet group alone.

Are the Ascendant and lunar nodes part of a stellium?

They are important chart points, but they are not planets. Count the planetary cluster first, then note whether an angle or node closely contacts it.
