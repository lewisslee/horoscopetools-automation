Find my birth chart:
start with the record.

To find your birth chart, gather your birth date, recorded local time and birth city, then use a calculator that shows its settings. If the time is missing, you can still inspect date-stable placements, but your houses and Ascendant should remain unresolved.

Last updated November 1, 2026

Find it in this order

01

Locate the primary record

Use a birth certificate, family record or other source that distinguishes a recorded time from a remembered estimate. Preserve uncertainty rather than silently rounding it away.

02

Choose one city and one setting set

Enter the birth city that matches the record, then note the visible zodiac and house-system choices. The setting labels are part of the calculation.

03

Read stable information before sensitive information

Start with displayed planetary positions, then add houses and Ascendant only when the time can support them. This order prevents an estimated horizon from becoming the story’s foundation.

This page’s original contribution is the search trail: keep the source record, entered city and visible settings together as one find-my-chart receipt. A saved image without those three pieces is difficult to reproduce or compare.

When your time is not on the record

Unknown: do not assign houses or an Ascendant.

Approximate: calculate a small range and compare only what stays stable.

Conflicting: keep both recorded candidates until you can resolve the source, rather than selecting the more appealing result.

These categories match the site’s calculator guidance. Use the Rising-sign two-result check for a defensible range, see the input map for what each field changes, and use the free birth chart calculator guide to read the result by its input dependency.

After you find a wheel

Read the displayed result in passes: validate the inputs, locate the coordinate, then limit the claim to what the calculation actually supports. The star-chart reading order explains that sequence.

A birth chart can be used as an interpretive framework; it is not a scientifically established prediction about your personality, choices or future.

Find-my-birth-chart questions

Can I find my birth chart with only a date?

You can generate a limited date-based result, but you should not treat houses or the Ascendant as confirmed without an adequately recorded time and place.

What city should I use?

Use the actual birth city in the record, not the current home city or the nearest famous city selected for convenience.

Why does the same date give different charts?

Time, local place and visible calculation settings can change portions of a chart. Compare those inputs before interpreting the difference.

Can I compare my chart with someone else’s?

Yes, but preserve both source records separately and start with one declared comparison question; see the synastry guide.
